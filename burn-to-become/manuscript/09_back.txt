@front QUESTIONS FOR REFLECTION AND DISCUSSION
These questions can be used alone with a notebook, with a friend, or in a reading group. There are no correct answers. The useful ones are the honest ones.
### Part I — The Life You Are Sleepwalking Through
1. Which of your current routines began as a choice and now feels like a fact?
2. What have you been “preparing” for longer than a year? What would contact with reality look like?
3. The book distinguishes recovery from avoidance. Where in your week does one disguise itself as the other?
4. Which of the four voices—fear, ego, approval, values—most often gets the final word in your decisions?
5. What would you lose if you stopped being “someone who could” and became “someone who did,” even imperfectly?
### Part II — The War Inside You
1. Describe a time when changing your environment worked better than trying harder.
2. What small promise to yourself do you break most often? What would a smaller, keepable version be?
3. Is there a label you use about yourself that sounds like an observation but works like a prediction?
4. Who is in your invisible audience? What would change if they were no longer watching?
5. Which mountain in your life might not be yours? How would you find out?
### Part III — The People Who Refused to Remain Small
1. Alexander could not recognize enough. What would “enough” look like in one area of your life?
2. Musashi’s legend is cleaner than his history. What legend do you tell about yourself that the evidence does not fully support?
3. Jordan’s Pistons years suggest that sometimes the answer to failure is a better system, not more intensity. Where might that apply to you?
4. Ronaldo became better by changing the teenager he was. What part of your younger self are you protecting in the name of authenticity?
5. Frankl insisted that meaning is not a formula for survival. Why does that caution matter?
### Part IV — The Cage and the Open Door
1. When does looking at other people’s lives inform you, and when does it hand you a deadline you never agreed to?
2. What in your life are you defending simply because it is already assembled?
3. If the door opened tomorrow, what would you build on the other side—and what would your ordinary Tuesday look like there?
### Part V — What the Fire Is For
1. What are you giving your life to that you have never consciously chosen?
2. What is one sunk cost you might be protecting at the expense of the future?
3. After a goal you achieved, what happened on the day after the photograph?
4. If you knew you had five years left, what would you stop postponing?
5. The Epilogue says: “Burn. But not yourself.” What does that mean in your life right now?

@front A NOTE ON THE ILLUSTRATIONS
Every illustration and typographic plate in this edition was created for it. None reproduces an existing photograph, painting, or manga panel.
While the book was being written, a private mood board of images collected online—many drawn from manga such as Takehiko Inoue’s Vagabond, a retelling of Musashi’s life, and Kentaro Miura’s Berserk, as well as anonymous motivational graphics—helped shape its atmosphere: black and white, high contrast, grain, feathers, stone, and slopes. Those images belong to their creators and are not reproduced here. The motifs they share—a figure climbing a slope, wings, a boulder pushed uphill, a door of light—are old ones, and they have been redrawn from scratch in this book’s own style.
The diagrams summarize ideas discussed in the text and are simplifications. They are meant to help you think, not to stand in for the research cited in the Notes.
If a future edition licenses artwork from a specific artist, it will be credited here.

### Chapter 23 — Musashi

@note Ganryūjima | The best-known account of the duel with Sasaki Kojirō, the Nitenki, was compiled in the eighteenth century, more than a century after the event.
@note ink paintings | Works attributed to Musashi, including Shrike on a Withered Branch (Koboku Meigeki-zu), are held in Japanese collections; the Izumi City Kubosō Memorial Museum of Art holds that painting. Attributions of some other works are debated.
