# Channel concept: RECEIPTS — "We test the viral claims so you don't have to."

Research snapshot: 2026-09-28. Nothing here is guaranteed to go viral. It is a plan built from what the research says audiences and the algorithm reward.

## 2. Why this concept
People are flooded with "hacks", AI tools and money claims and cannot tell which ones work. The wish underneath that is *"tell me the truth, fast."* RECEIPTS turns it into a binge loop.

**The mechanism (legible in 3 seconds):** one viral claim, one real test with a constraint, and a stamped verdict: **VERIFIED**, **BUSTED** or **IT'S COMPLICATED**, with the actual receipts (screenshots, numbers, timers) on screen.

Why it works on each lever:
- **Curiosity gap:** the verdict is hidden until the end, and the claim itself is the hook ("Can AI really replace a $60k job in a week?").
- **Proof:** on-screen receipts satisfy the "show me" instinct and reduce satisfaction-survey risk.
- **Participation:** viewers submit the next claim. Their name goes in the credits and the top-voted claim gets tested.
- **Binge structure:** a running **Scoreboard** (verified vs busted count) is shown in every video and pinned in a playlist. Viewers want to see it tick.
- **Shorts loop:** a stamp slam is a natural, rewatchable payoff.
- **Faceless-capable:** it works with screen capture, voiceover and captions.

## 3. Content rules (these protect the channel)
1. **Every result must be real.** No staged, faked or cherry-picked outcomes. If a test fails to reproduce, that is the video.
2. Show methodology and sample size on screen. Say when the test is too small to be conclusive.
3. No financial or medical advice. Money and health claims are tested as "does the claim's mechanic work", with a disclaimer, and health tests stay non-clinical. Be cautious with anything involving real health outcomes or other people's money.
4. Don't imitate other creators' branding, and don't misrepresent people or companies.
5. Packaging never promises more than the video delivers.

## 4. Formats
**Long-form (8–14 min, weekly):** one claim, a full test, the verdict.

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Cold open on the sharpest moment (the claim vs the result, blurred). |
| 0:05–0:20 | The claim, the constraint and the bet. |
| 0:20–1:30 | Set-up: rules, budget, timer, and what would count as VERIFIED. |
| 1:30–8:00 | The test in chapters. Each chapter ends on a mini-cliffhanger, with a scoreboard for the claim's sub-parts. |
| 8:00–9:30 | Twist: an unexpected result or a counter-test. |
| 9:30–10:30 | Verdict stamp and the receipts. |
| Last 15s | Scoreboard update, the next claim (chosen from comments), end-screen to the previous verdict video. |

**Shorts (30–45s, 4–5 per long video):**
1. Claim, then a stamp slam verdict in the first 8s ("This viral hack? BUSTED."), then 25s of why.
2. "Scoreboard update": counter animation, then the next test teaser.
3. Best moment, cut from the long video, with a caption question.
4. "Claim vs reality" side-by-side.
End Shorts on a frame that connects to the first frame to encourage loops.

## 6. Sample cold open (episode 1, fill in real results)
> [Screen: a viral thumbnail claim. Stamp hovers over it, unstamped.]
> "This video has [X] million views and promises [claim]. I'm going to follow it exactly, with real money and a real timer. Whatever happens, you'll see every receipt."
> [Cut] "By day three, something went wrong." [Hard cut to title.]

Only use a "[X] million views" or similar figure if it's the true, verified number at recording time.

## 7. Thumbnails and titles
- Layout: the claim in 3 words, the stamp (green / red / amber), one clear image. High contrast, readable at phone size.
- Title formula: *"[Viral claim] — Tested"* or *"I Tried [claim] for [time]"* under about 55 characters.
- Use Test & Compare on three thumbnail options per video.

## 8. 30-day launch plan
| Week | Output |
|---|---|
| 1 | Channel set-up, playlist "The Scoreboard", pick 6 claims, check search demand for each. Film episodes 1–2. |
| 2 | Publish episode 1 plus 4 Shorts. Start a community post asking for claims. Film episode 3. |
| 3 | Publish episode 2 plus Shorts. Reply to every early comment. Review first-30s retention. |
| 4 | Publish episode 3. First Scoreboard update. Decide on the first pivot from the data. |

## 9. Metrics and decision rules
- Hook: retention at 0:30 (target 70%+; if below, rewrite the opening before anything else).
- Packaging: impressions click-through rate. If below about 4%, change the thumbnail first.
- Satisfaction and depth: average % viewed, plus the share of viewers who watch a second video.
- Participation: claims submitted per video, comments per 1,000 views.
- After 10 uploads, keep the top-quartile formats and drop the bottom.

## 10. Risks
- Crowded space: "I tested X" is popular, so differentiation depends on real receipts, a consistent verdict format and the Scoreboard.
- Real effort: each test takes actual time and sometimes money. Budget for it.
- Legal and safety: avoid defamation, false claims, health or financial advice, and undisclosed sponsorships.
