## Fact-check notes (the film hedges these)
- **Demo (Oct 15, 2026):** Tesla unveiled its next-generation Roadster at a SpaceX test site in McGregor, Texas (CNBC, CNN, Axios, Fox Business, as summarised in search results). Reports describe a driverless car climbing a curved ramp, hanging upside down, flipping and hovering briefly on air jets before landing. Musk called it a flying car; he claimed 0–60 mph in under one second (Tesla's timer) and said production is planned for 2028; no price was announced; he said "This is not necessarily a safety-optimized car." Street-legal status was reported as unclear. **Not independently verified by me; I could not open the articles, only search summaries.**
- **Expert quote:** Roger Connor, Smithsonian curator: "We are literally talking about hopping" (IBTimes). The film adds "no sustained flight was shown" per reports.
- **2017 promises:** unveiled Nov 2017: 1.9 s 0–60, 250+ mph top speed, 620-mile range, $200,000 base; $50,000 reservation, $250,000 Founder Series paid in full; production promised for 2020; later dates touted: 2022, 2023, 2024 (sources: Autocar, Green Car Reports, HotHardware, Electrek). Feb 2024: Musk said the design goal was 0–60 in under 1 second. The SpaceX thruster package was first mentioned in 2018.
- **Flying car history:** Taylor Aerocar first flew 1949, certified airworthy 1956, six built; manufacturer deal needed 500 orders, got about half; a 1970s Ford study found demand but regulation/safety concerns stopped it (Plane & Pilot, HistoryLink). Alef: FAA approval to fly as an experimental aircraft (exact date not verified). Joby is furthest in FAA certification, Archer behind; no full type certification in the sources found.
- **"Money won't matter in 2036":** The Economist interview with editor-in-chief Zanny Minton Beddoes at Tesla's Texas Gigafactory, published late July 2026. He expects deflation, supports "universal high income", and said "the Treasury should just simply issue people checks." Earlier: Nov 2025 (U.S.–Saudi Investment Forum): work optional, money irrelevant in 10–20 years; Dec 2025 X post: "Civilization will either be gone or AI/robotics will eliminate scarcity. Either way, money won't matter." Condition: he said that barring something like a global war, "we're headed for an age of amazing abundance." (Sources: TheStreet, LADbible, Yahoo Finance, Forbes commentary, Hardware Busters/Economist transcript summary.)
- **Criticism:** Forbes (David Birnbaum, Aug 2026) argues scarcity of physical resources and ownership still make money matter; Gautam Mukunda argues Musk underrates relative status needs. The film says "critics argue".
- **Jobs:** Yale Budget Lab: no discernible disruption in the broad labor market 33 months after ChatGPT (late-2025 data; may have changed).
- **Optimus:** per The Information via Electrek/others, output rose from a few dozen/week in Q2 2026 to several hundred in August; not confirmed by Tesla; Musk wants public sales by end of 2027; a 2025 target of ~10,000 robots was missed.
- **Self-driving:** Waymo reported above 500,000 rides/week (Sept 2026); Musk said in April 2026 Tesla's robotaxis would likely "not be super material this year". Tesla's current city count could not be verified, so the film does not give one.
- **OpenResearch study:** about 1,000 people got $1,000/month for 3 years vs a control group ($50/month); worked about 1.3 hours/week less; spent more on essentials; no clear gain in reported physical health. Sources differ on sample size.
- **Keynes (1930):** predicted living standards 4–8× higher in 100 years and far shorter work hours; the wealth arrived, hours fell much less. "15-hour week" is popular shorthand; the essay says three-hour shifts.
- **Track record:** 2016 LA→NYC self-drive by end of 2017 not done as promised; Jan 2025 "boy who cried wolf" admission; July 2025 robotaxis for "half the population of the U.S." by year end; April 2026 caution (Electrek, CleanTechnica, Reuters via WHBL). SpaceX's first orbital-class booster landing was in Dec 2015 (general knowledge, not searched here).
- **Not used / unverified:** the "$16.3 billion a day" wealth-loss headline; any Tesla robotaxi city count; a 10–20% "killer robot" probability (single secondary summary); the 2036 interview's exact wording beyond the quotes above.
