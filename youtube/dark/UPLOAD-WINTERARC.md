## Fact-check notes (the film hedges these)
- **Origin:** creator Carly Berges (@carlyupgraded) is credited by Today.com with the Oct 1 ~90-day "laser-focused" framing; sources date it to fall 2022 and dispute whether the phrase or the challenge came first. The anime/manga "arc" origin of the name is a guess (Know Your Meme). It became a meme in 2024 ("double XP event").
- **Fresh start effect:** Dai, Milkman & Riis (2014, *Management Science*): Google searches for "diet", gym visits and goal commitments rise after temporal landmarks (start of week/month, birthday, day after a holiday).
- **Norcross resolutions study:** 200 resolution-makers; reported 55% still going after 1 month, 40% after 6 months, 19% after 2 years (as reported by APA and The New Republic). The film calls them "reported figures".
- **Burnout:** the film cites one student's account (a student-debate article) and general critiques of hustle culture. It does not claim winter arcs cause burnout in general.
- **Lally et al.:** median ~66 days to a stable habit, range 18–254 days; the study ran 84 days so the longest times are model projections; a single missed day did not seem to ruin habit formation (secondary reports).
- **If-then plans:** Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006): 94 tests, medium-to-large average effect on goal attainment (d ≈ 0.65). d is a standardised effect size, not "65% more likely".
- **Progress monitoring:** Harkin et al. (2016, *Psychological Bulletin*): 138 randomised studies, 19,951 people, goal attainment d ≈ 0.40; stronger when progress is written down or shared; most studies were health goals.
- **Sleep:** AASM/SRS consensus: adults should get 7+ hours regularly.
- **Movement:** WHO 2020: 150–300 min/week moderate (or 75–150 vigorous), plus muscle strengthening on 2+ days. Singh et al. (2023, *BJSM*): umbrella review of 97 reviews, medium effects on depression and anxiety versus usual care. The "1.5x better than medication" press claim is not used.
- **Light / SAD:** estimated prevalence from about 0.4% to 10% depending on place and measurement; morning light is the most commonly recommended light-therapy timing. Specific dosing is not given in the film.
- **Cold showers:** Buijze et al. (2016, *PLoS ONE*): 3,018 participants, 30 days, 29% lower self-reported sickness absence, no difference in illness days.
- **5 AM:** the film makes no claim about waking times beyond "sleep enough, pick a time you can keep".
- **Not verified, not used:** Strava "Quitter's Day"; the "21 days" origin story.
