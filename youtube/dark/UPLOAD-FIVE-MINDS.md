How we know (general information, simplified for a short film):
- Bison: wildlife agencies such as the US National Park Service describe bison walking into storms, which shortens the time they spend in them, while cattle tend to drift with the storm. Adult bulls can weigh close to a ton and run about 35 mph. In winter they sweep snow aside with their heads to reach grass.
- Peregrine falcon: dives of around 240 mph (about 390 km/h) have been recorded, the fastest of any animal. It gains height first, then folds its wings and stoops.
- Sharks: the shark lineage is more than 400 million years old; the first trees appeared about 385 million years ago. Many species, such as the great white, need to keep swimming so water flows over their gills; others can pump water. Sharks sense the tiny electric fields of other animals with organs called the ampullae of Lorenzini.
- Fox: red foxes live in forests, deserts, mountains and cities, and eat whatever is available. They can hear small animals moving under snow. One study (Cerveny et al., 2011) found that fox pounces were more successful in a particular compass direction; the idea that they use the Earth's magnetic field is a hypothesis, not settled science.
- Ants: ants can carry many times their own weight (about 10 to 50 times, depending on the species). Ants have been on Earth for more than 100 million years, with over 12,000 described species. Trails are marked with chemical signals that get stronger as more ants use them.
