## Fact-check notes (the film hedges these)
- Global average social media time: 141 minutes/day (Feb 2025, DataReportal figures as republished by Statista). 141 min × 365 × 50 yrs ≈ 1,787 days ≈ 4.9 years nonstop, ≈ 7.3 years of waking time (16 waking h/day), ≈ 9% of 80 years. This is arithmetic, not a forecast.
- "Brain rot": Oxford University Press Word of the Year 2024; OUP reported usage up 230% year on year.
- Aza Raskin: his own estimate, "on the order of a hundred thousand human lifetimes per day" (TED/Adam Grant transcript). Other write-ups cite different figures (e.g. 200,000); treat as a rough estimate.
- Gloria Mark (UC Irvine): about 2.5 min on one screen in 2004, about 47 sec in later studies. This is time on a screen before switching, not an attention-span limit; the 2004 sample was small.
- Texas Tech survey (1,100 US adults, Health Communication): about 16.5% reported severely problematic bad-news consumption; self-reported.
- UCL study (1,000+ participants): link runs both ways between mood and seeking negative content.
- Facebook emotional contagion study (Kramer, Guillory, Hancock; PNAS, June 2014): small effects, criticized over consent. The film does not cite participant counts.
- Slot-machine comparison and "attention favors threats" are widely discussed explanatory framings, not single-study claims.
- Not verified, so not used: the word's exact origin/first use; the 2023 Surgeon General "3+ hours doubles risk" claim (kept out of the film).
- The six steps are practical suggestions, not clinical advice.
