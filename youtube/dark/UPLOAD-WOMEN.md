## Description
A thought experiment, not a prediction: at 7:42 one Tuesday morning, every woman and girl on Earth disappears. About four billion people. What would happen hour by hour, day by day, and over a century, using real data on health care, schools, unpaid care work, farming, the economy and human reproduction?

The film is not about men being unable to do these jobs. They could learn them. It is about scale and speed, and about how much of the world's daily life quietly depends on women, often unseen and unpaid.

SOURCES (verify yourself)
- UN World Population Prospects / Demographic Yearbook: women are about 49.7% of the world population (about 4.0 billion of 8.1 billion)
- WHO (2019), "Delivered by Women, Led by Men: A Gender and Equity Analysis of the Global Health and Social Workforce": women about 70% of the health and social care workforce; 24 million of 28.5 million nurses and midwives; about a quarter of senior roles (secondary reporting; check the report)
- UNESCO / Teacher Task Force: women about 94% of pre-primary teachers and roughly two thirds of primary teachers worldwide
- ILO (2018), "Care work and care jobs for the future of decent work": 16.4 billion hours of unpaid care work per day; women do 76.2%; about 9% of global GDP (US$11 trillion at minimum wage, 2011 PPP)
- FAO (SOFA 2010-11 and "The Role of Women in Agriculture"): women about 43% (just over 40% in some tables) of the agricultural labour force in developing countries
- ILO: women are roughly two in five of the world's workers (about 40%)
- Artificial wombs: 2017 lamb study (Children's Hospital of Philadelphia); FDA advisory committee (2023) discussed trials for extremely premature infants only; no technology supports full human gestation outside the body
- Global life expectancy about 73 years (UN)
