## The film (5:44)
No infographics. A slow, painted short film with one figure, one path and one choice at five in the morning. It is built on a true fact: **"disciple" and "discipline" share the Latin root *discere*, "to learn."**

| Time | Scene | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **5:00 AM** | Alarm. Cold room, warm bed. The clock slips to 5:03, 5:07, 5:12 while the figure sits. "Nobody would ever know. You would know. This is where it is decided." |
| 0:32 | **Title** | DISCIPLE, with the definition: *discipulus*, a learner |
| 0:40 | **The root** | DISCIPLE and DISCIPLINE, both from *discere*: a disciple is a learner; discipline is what a learner does until the learning is true |
| 1:10 | **The path** | A field. Day 1 the grass stands back up. Day 2 it leans. Day 7 a faint line. Day 30 anyone could see it. Day 100 you could not miss it. Day 365. "A path is what is left when a walk is repeated." |
| 2:02 | **Weather and path** | A jagged "how it felt" line against 30 days of "what you did". Step back and the jagged line flattens; the dots remain. Motivation is weather; discipline is the path |
| 2:26 | **The drop and the stone** | *Gutta cavat lapidem*, "the drop hollows the stone" (a Latin line found in Ovid), shown over 10,000 years: not by force, by falling often |
| 2:56 | **Back to the room** | Five more minutes. You were tired yesterday. It will not matter today. "Do not negotiate. The first step is not the work. Put your feet on the floor." Dawn comes as the figure stands |
| 3:34 | **The missed days** | Day 101 to 108 missed; the grass creeps back; "it is over." It is not: a path is not erased in a day. "Never miss twice." Day 109, back |
| 4:10 | **Four seasons** | Spring, summer, autumn, winter, the same walk. "Discipline is a kind of love: the kind that keeps showing up after the feeling has gone." |
| 4:40 | **The hill** | Someone far behind is walking where you walked. A disciple learns by following; a teacher is a disciple who did not stop |
| 5:10 | **One small thing** | Small enough for your worst day. Same time, same place. Never miss twice. Then walk. "Tell me your one thing in the comments." |
| 5:34 | End card | |

What is different from the bulletins: widescreen cinema bars, painted scenes, a deep British narrator at a slower pace with long silences, plain serif subtitles that light word by word, slow dissolves instead of whooshes, and a score that rises from almost nothing at 5 a.m. to its fullest on the hill. Nothing is hidden. It still has a useful ending (the three rules).

Honest limits: the figures are simple silhouettes (stick-limbed, not hand-drawn animation), the pace is deliberately slow, and I cannot hear the audio. Please listen once, especially to the narrator's voice and speed; I can swap the voice (a different male voice, female, faster or slower) and re-encode in about 10 minutes without re-rendering the picture. "A new reveal every second" does not apply here by design: this film trades density for mood.

Disciple and discipline are the same word. Both come from the Latin discere: to learn. A short film about the smallest decision at five in the morning, and why a path is only what is left when a walk is repeated.

DISCIPLE: a short film about discipline.

The three rules from the film:
1. Pick one small thing, small enough to do on your worst day.
2. Same time. Same place.
3. Never miss twice.
Then walk. Tell me your one thing in the comments.

Etymology: disciple and discipline both derive from Latin discipulus (pupil) and disciplina (instruction), from discere (to learn). "Gutta cavat lapidem" ("the drop hollows the stone") is a Latin line found in Ovid; the film shows it over invented timescales (years 1 to 10,000) as an illustration.

A work of fiction and a reflection, not advice. Gentle light changes only. No rapid flashing, no gore and no jump scares. Narration is a synthetic voice.
