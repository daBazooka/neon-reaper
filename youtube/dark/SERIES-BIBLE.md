# THE BUREAU OF HOUSEHOLD ATTENTION — series bible

An original analog-horror series styled as a lost 1990s "home safety" tape. Its subject is the attention economy: the glow you are looking at right now. There are no monsters, no faces, no children, no gore and no jump scares. The horror is recognition.

**The "rare yet working" choice:** analog horror is proven, and the generic version is saturated. The gap is analog horror *as allegory of a universal, current fear* (the screen itself), with layered, solvable hidden messages and a format that feeds the session-contribution signal (an "autoplay" ending).

## 2. Concept
Fictional "Bureau of Household Attention" bulletins teach households how to live with "the Glow". Each rule sounds like calm safety advice. Each is a real attention-economy behaviour seen from outside, and each is a little worse than the last.

| Rule | Surface | What it is really about |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The glow is warm. Sit closer. | Comfort | Engagement pull; the screen as a warm place. |
| 2. If the screen asks if you are still watching, always answer yes. | A polite prompt | Autoplay friction; the "NO" button is nearly invisible. |
| 3. Do not count the hours. The clock is not on your side. | Time advice | Time distortion; "watch time" counters. |
| 4. Your neighbours are watching too. This is comforting. | Reassurance | Social proof; a whole block of silhouettes in blue light. |
| 5. If someone speaks to you, say "one minute." Then do not stop. | Etiquette | Neglecting the people beside you. |
| 6. Do not look up. | A warning | The exit: literally "LOOK UP". |
| Finale | "This bulletin ends when you stop watching." | The viewer *is* the subject. Ends in an autoplay countdown with no cancel. |

Tone: quiet, procedural, sad. It never lectures. The final message is an invitation, not a scare: look up.

## 4. Engagement mechanics (why this should be re-watchable)
- **Cold, clear premise:** "Rules for living with the glow" is a proven list format (a rule per beat, so each rule can also stand alone as a Short).
- **Pause-and-scrub reward:** hidden text is placed where enthusiasts look (dark regions, paused frames, spectrogram).
- **Comment engine:** pin a comment: *"This tape contains 4 hidden messages. Timestamps in the replies get pinned."* Pin correct findings (with credit) to reward solvers and keep threads alive.
- **Session contribution:** the ending is an autoplay countdown that leads into the next video (Bulletin 02 or the playlist) via the end screen.
- **Series glue:** the answer to H1/H2 ("LOOK UP") becomes the first word of a series-wide phrase. Each bulletin hides one more word.

## 6. Production facts
- 4:3 tape frame in 1080p (long) and full-frame 9:16 crops (Shorts). Everything is procedural: silhouettes, rooms, window grids, clock, dialog UI, logo.
- VHS pass: horizontal wobble, colour bleed, scanlines, grain, tracking band, head-switch noise, bloom, vignette. Degradation increases through the tape.
- Audio: fully synthesised. Room tone, mains hum, a growing low drone, a jingle that is corrupted later, clock ticks that drift, a typewriter-key sound for captions, a tape-stop, and the hidden spectrogram text.
- No voice acting. All narration is on-screen typed text, which suits the genre and works muted.

## 7. Honest limits
- I cannot hear the audio and have only checked it numerically, and by rendering the hidden spectrogram text as an image. Please listen once, especially to the jingle and the drone, and tell me what to adjust.
- The look is procedural vector art with a strong analog treatment. It is not hand-painted studio animation. Its strength is atmosphere and writing, not character acting.
- Nothing guarantees a hit. The niche has a proven audience, but novelty fades. Watch the first-30s retention and comment rate of Bulletin 01 and iterate.
