The one real idea (optional reading)
Researchers who study procrastination (Sirois & Pychyl, 2013) describe it as a way of managing a bad mood, not as laziness: we switch to something that feels better right now, and the cost lands on the future self. A well-studied way to start anyway is an "if-then" plan that says when and where you will begin (implementation intentions; Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006, average effect about d = 0.65 across 94 tests, which is a medium-to-large effect, not a promise). Example: "If it is 9:00 and I am at my desk, I will write one sentence." Then see what happens in two minutes.
